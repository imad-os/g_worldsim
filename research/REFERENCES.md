# References and how each was used

| Technique | Source | Used? | Why / why not |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goal-Oriented Action Planning | J. Orkin, "Three States and a Plan: The AI of F.E.A.R.", GDC 2006; "Applying Goal-Oriented Action Planning to Games", AI Game Programming Wisdom 2, 2004 | **Yes** (`sim/goap.ts`) | Composes existing skills toward goals nobody scripted; actions come from data (recipes). Proven in shipped games, cheap with small fact sets. |
| Additive heuristic for planning | B. Bonet, H. Geffner, "Planning as heuristic search", Artificial Intelligence 129, 2001 (HSP) | **Yes**, stock-aware variant | Guides A* well on resource chains; inadmissibility only costs a little optimality. |
| Utility AI (response curves) | D. Mark, *Behavioral Mathematics for Game AI*, 2009; D. Mark & K. Dill, "Improving AI Decision Modeling Through Utility Theory", GDC 2010 | Kept (Brain) + **drives** in Colony | The existing needs layer already is utility AI; goals are scored the same way, and plan cost / success chance enter the score. |
| Contract Net Protocol | R. G. Smith, "The Contract Net Protocol", IEEE Trans. Computers C-29, 1980 | **Yes**, one round, centralised | Task allocation by bids (fit, cost, chance) with crews; cheap and deterministic. |
| Hierarchical Task Networks | K. Erol, J. Hendler, D. Nau, "HTN planning: complexity and expressivity", AAAI 1994; SHOP2 (Nau et al. 2003) | Not now | Needs hand-written methods per task, which is what the owner wants to avoid; GOAP finds the decompositions itself. Good fit later for multi-person plans (backlog). |
| Behavior Trees | M. Colledanchise, P. Ögren, *Behavior Trees in Robotics and AI*, 2018 | No | Good for authored reactive behaviour; here the reactive layer is already utility-based and the gap was deliberation. |
| Plan repair / replanning | M. Fox et al., "Plan stability: replanning versus plan repair", ICAPS 2006 | **Yes**, simple | One re-plan from the current state after a failure, with the failed action now dearer. |
| Laplace rule of succession, Beta-Bernoulli | standard; see also R. Sutton, A. Barto, *Reinforcement Learning*, 2nd ed. 2018 (bandits, ch. 2) | **Yes** | Success chance per action with a uniform prior; optimism for untried actions. |
| Exponential back-off | standard (Ethernet, retries) | **Yes** | Stops behavioural loops on goals that keep failing without forbidding them for good. |
| Social / cultural learning | R. Boyd, P. Richerson, *Culture and the Evolutionary Process*, 1985 | **Yes**, simple | The camp's experience counts at half weight in each person's estimates. |
| Reinforcement learning (Q-learning, policy gradients) | Sutton & Barto 2018 | Not now | Needs many episodes and a fixed state encoding; the world changes, runs are long, and plans + outcome statistics give most of the benefit at a fraction of the cost. Candidate for learning drive weights per camp (backlog). |
| Evolutionary algorithms | J. Holland, *Adaptation in Natural and Artificial Systems*, 1975; K. Sims, "Evolving Virtual Creatures", SIGGRAPH 1994 | Partly already | Traits are inherited with mutation (`life.ts`); selection pressure is weak. Evolving drive weights / temperaments is in the backlog. |
| Constraint satisfaction for layout | R. Dechter, *Constraint Processing*, 2003; P. Merrell et al., "Computer-generated residential building layouts", SIGGRAPH Asia 2010 | **Yes**, generate-and-test with scoring | Hard constraints (space, access, apron, inside the wall) + soft scores (door facing the centre, compactness), with flood-fill access checks. Full CSP solvers are not needed for 5 x 5 huts. |
| Flood fill / connectivity | standard BFS | **Yes** | Enclosure ("is it a room?") and access ("can you get to every door?"). |
| Minimum enclosing circle | E. Welzl, "Smallest enclosing disks", 1991 | Not yet | The wall is a circle round the camp centre sized by area; Welzl's circle (or a convex hull wall) would use less material. Backlog. |
| Settlement location | central place / resource-weighted site selection (e.g. W. Christaller 1933; agent-based archaeology such as Kohler & Gumerman, *Dynamics in Human and Primate Societies*, 2000) | **Yes**, simple score | Open ground, water, wood, distance. |
| Generative agents (LLM memory, reflection) | J. S. Park et al., "Generative Agents", UIST 2023 | Already (planner.ts) | The LLM layer keeps notes, summaries, relations; the local layer now also keeps skill knowledge that prompts could use (backlog). |
| Flow fields / navigation meshes | E. Emerson, "Crowd pathfinding and steering using flow field tiles", *Game AI Pro*, 2013 | Not now | A* per agent costs ~30 searches per game hour at 5 people; worth it only for big crowds or zombie hordes (backlog). |
